Outside data

Who's renting: homestead share by license type

Exact matches only. Homestead means the owner says they live there.

Non-commercial rentals sit on a homestead parcel 75–78% of the time. Commercial-owner rentals: 4%.

That's the split this tool cares about for its second user, the young or small landlord. Someone renting out half of a double they live in looks very different on the roll from an investor running whole-house commercial rentals. The final version can point each one to the right exemption and license rules.

Not in the chatbot yet. Right now it's the stress test for the address matcher: see how 4,299 real addresses matched.

How it shaped the design

Half of households own, so homeowners are the main user. A $55K median income and no finance background means an 8th-grade reading level, short answers, and the math hidden until asked for.

Why the senior freeze is front and center

About 1 in 6 residents is 65+. The freeze is the biggest thing an older owner might be missing, so the bill card flags it on every homestead home without one.

Checking the roll against the Census

The Census median home value ($296,400, owner-reported) sits about $50K above the roll's median homestead value ($244,800, Jan 2023 values). See chart 3. The Census Geocoder also confirmed 234 of 258 addresses the matcher missed (details).

Census block groups (course data)

The course Box folder (posted Nov 2, 2026) has a crosswalk that places each parcel on the 2026 roll in its 2020 census block group, along with American Community Survey (ACS 2020–2024) numbers for each of the 439 block groups. A block group is a few city blocks, about 600 to 3,000 people.

How the tool uses it: after a lookup, each home's card shows its block group's median income, how many homes are owner-occupied, median rent, and what residents say homes are worth. The card labels these as neighborhood numbers, not facts about that house.

Can it be matched reliably? 86% of parcels have a block group (140,850 of 163,135). The other 14%, mostly condos and parcels without a usable address, show no Census card. Block-group survey numbers also have wide margins of error, so the tool shows them as context only.

My check: I recomputed the median home value for each block group from the roll and the crosswalk. It matched the course file within 1% for 98.6% of 428 block groups (exact for 87.9%). The small gaps come from slightly different definitions of "residential."

Across every income level the roll sits 22–28% below what residents say their homes are worth, with the biggest gap in the lowest-income block groups. That fits 2023 values versus 2020–2024 survey answers. A neighborhood model in the same analysis explains 75% of the variation in block-group home values. Education is the strongest single predictor (R² 0.70 alone vs. 0.39 for income), and the patterns reflect the city's history of segregation and lending, not just one factor.
