Address matching

People type addresses like "2808 St. Charles Avenue." The tax roll stores "2808 ST CHARLES AV." The matcher (matcher.js, which runs right in your browser) translates one into the other and finds the parcel, without sending the address anywhere.

How it works

  1. Clean it upUppercase, strip punctuation, "New Orleans, LA," and the ZIP. A ZIP outside 701xx or a city like Metairie means outside the parish.
  2. Pull out the partsHouse number (first number of a range like 1816-1818), unit (Apt, Unit, #), and street suffix: Avenue → AV, Boulevard → BL.
  3. Translate namesSpell names the way the roll does. N/S/E/W gets dropped because the roll drops it.
  4. Look it upNumber + street name, narrowed by suffix and unit. No exact hit? Try a 1–2 letter typo fix, then a house number 2–4 away (doubles).
  5. Ask if unsureSeveral parcels match? The owner picks. North and South Broad both have a "1000 BROAD ST."

Cross-check with the Census Geocoder

I sent the 258 misses (the 251 above plus my hand-test misses) to the U.S. Census Geocoder batch API. If the Census couldn't find them either, the problem would be bad input. Instead, 234 came back as real addresses with coordinates.

So the misses are about how the roll numbers parcels, not about bad addresses. Next step for the final version: snap those geocoded points to the City's parcel map to catch condo towers filed under one number.

API: geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/locations/addressbatch (free, no key).
