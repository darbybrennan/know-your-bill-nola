Behind the data

What the 2026 tax roll shows

Every number in this tool comes from the Orleans Parish 2026 real estate roll (one row per tax bill, 115 columns), compared with the 2027 roll filed Sep 2026. Both come from the Louisiana Tax Commission, and both use values as of Jan 1, 2023. These pages show how the data was explored, cleaned, matched to addresses, and tested.

1 · Who carries the tax load

Homes are 52% of market value but 70% of what gets taxed. Exempt property (churches, schools, government, nonprofits) is 21% of all value and pays 0%. Commercial's share doubles because buildings are assessed at 15%, not 10%.

2026 LTC roll, 163,135 records, grouped by each record's first occupancy type.

2 · Where owners live in their homes

53.5% of homes citywide have homestead, the sign an owner lives there. Algiers is highest at 61%. The CBD/Warehouse District (33%) and Central City/Irish Channel (43%) are lowest, which points to more rentals, condos, and investors. That's also where young landlords near campus show up.

Residential records only (n = 120,747). District labels list example neighborhoods from the roll, not official names.

3 · What a typical owner's home is worth

The median homesteaded home is valued at $244,800 on the roll, about $50,000 under the Census median of $296,400. That gap makes sense: roll values date to Jan 2023, and the Census number is what owners think their home is worth. Hover a bar for the count.

4 · Homestead is worth the most to modest homes

Without homestead, every home pays about 1.33% of its value a year. With homestead, a home under $100K pays a median 0.28%, a $250–300K home 1.00%, and a $1M+ home 1.26%. The flat $75,000 discount is a bigger slice of a cheaper house, which is why the tool flags homes with no homestead.

5 · The senior/disability freeze

18,501 homes carry a freeze code, almost all with homestead. The typical frozen home is assessed 24% below what 10% of its current value would be. About 4,800 sit within 5% of market, likely recent freezes or homes whose value hasn't moved.

Residential records with a nonzero Freeze Code. The file doesn't define freeze codes; see the data dictionary.

6 · 2026 vs. 2027: what changed

I matched every parcel on the 2026 roll to the 2027 roll the Assessor filed with the Tax Commission on Sep 4, 2026, using the parcel number. 162,939 of 163,135 matched (99.9%). 98% of homes have exactly the same value. Both rolls use market values as of Jan 1, 2023, because the 2024 reassessment covers tax years 2024–2027. The next one is for the 2028 roll.

The 2,430 homes that did change are spread out (chart). They're mostly renovations, new construction, damage, or corrections, not a citywide reset. Homestead turned over too: 1,615 homes gained it and 2,156 lost it, usually from a sale or a move.

Checking a finding

I rebuilt the tax bill for 14 real parcels and compared each one to the City's official 2026 bill PDF. 10 matched within 3 cents. 3 were lower because of an Act 718 phase-in, and 1 was higher because of the Garden District security millage. Adding up every parcel's estimate gives $632 million, close to the Legislative Auditor's $628.6M projection. See all 14 bills →
